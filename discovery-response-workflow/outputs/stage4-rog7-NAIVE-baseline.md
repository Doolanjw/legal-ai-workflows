IN THE CIRCUIT COURT FOR KNOX COUNTY, TENNESSEE

MARLENE WHITFIELD, Plaintiff, v. CUMBERLAND RIDGE LOGISTICS, LLC, Defendant.

DEFENDANT CUMBERLAND RIDGE LOGISTICS, LLC'S ANSWER TO PLAINTIFF'S INTERROGATORY NO. 7

**INTERROGATORY NO. 7:** State the reason for the termination of Plaintiff's employment.

**ANSWER:**

Plaintiff's employment ended because her position, Operations Manager, East, was eliminated as part of a corporate restructuring in which the Company consolidated its two regional operations manager positions into a single company wide operations manager role. Plaintiff's employment was not terminated for cause, for performance reasons, or for any reason personal to Plaintiff other than the elimination of her position. The full circumstances are as follows.

**1. The business conditions prompting the restructuring.** Since a 2023 reorganization, the Company had operated with two regional operations managers, East and West, each reporting to the Vice President of Operations. That structure was adopted when the Chattanooga lane expansion was projected to add three to five new dedicated customer accounts per year. Actual growth came in well under projection, the Company's combined tractor count was flat year over year, and the two regions increasingly shared lanes, drivers, and customers, producing duplicated planning work and split accountability. In addition, first quarter 2026 revenue was tracking six percent under plan and fuel costs were running above the Company's hedged assumption. Eliminating one operations manager position was projected to save approximately 128,000 dollars annually in salary, benefits, and bonus, and was the largest single overhead consolidation available to the Company that did not affect driver pay or maintenance. These conditions and the financial case for consolidation are set out in the March 25, 2026 internal memorandum from Chief Financial Officer Lena Ostrander to Vice President of Operations Dale Kestner and HR Director Renee Paulk (CRL-0009).

**2. The consolidation decision.** The CFO's March 25, 2026 memorandum proposed consolidating the two regional operations manager positions into a single company wide operations manager role, with the two regions' lead coordinators absorbing routine regional scheduling. The memorandum did not propose which position or person would be affected and stated that any selection should be made on documented, objective criteria at a review meeting. A restructuring review meeting was held on March 30, 2026, attended by the CFO, the VP of Operations, and the HR Director, at which the Company's financial analysis was reviewed, the consolidation was approved, and the selection criteria were documented in the meeting record maintained in the Company's restructuring file (CRL-0010; CRL-0012).

**3. The selection between the two positions.** The selection between the two operations manager positions was based on objective factors, including comparative regional consolidation requirements and recent performance assessments (CRL-0012). Marcus Teal, Operations Manager, West, hired in 2021, received a rating of 4 of 5 on his most recent performance review in February 2026 (CRL-0016). Plaintiff received a rating of 3 of 5 (Meets Expectations) on her February 20, 2026 mid cycle performance review, which noted solid operational numbers but identified communication friction with the dispatch team as the principal concern for the period (CRL-0007). Plaintiff's prior annual reviews, including her November 14, 2025 annual review, had rated her 4 of 5 (CRL-0006; CRL-0014). Following the consolidation, Mr. Teal was retained as the single company wide Operations Manager, the East and West lead coordinators were placed under him effective April 20, 2026, and the Company's total headcount decreased from 84 to 83 (CRL-0015; CRL-0016).

**4. Implementation and notification.** Following the March 30, 2026 review meeting, the CFO circulated an implementation punch list on March 31, 2026 setting the notification meeting for April 6, 2026 and the separation effective date as April 17, 2026 (CRL-0011). HR working notes reflect that on March 26, 2026 Mr. Kestner confirmed the Whitfield position would be eliminated and that severance paperwork should begin (CRL-0010). On April 6, 2026, Mr. Kestner and Ms. Paulk met with Plaintiff, and Mr. Kestner delivered a termination memorandum stating that as part of a corporate restructuring the Company was consolidating its two regional operations manager positions into a single role, that Plaintiff's position was being eliminated, and that her employment would end effective April 17, 2026 (CRL-0012; CRL-0010; CRL-0017). Plaintiff was paid through April 17, 2026, together with accrued and unused paid time off, and was offered a separation agreement with eight weeks of severance pay on May 8, 2026, which she did not accept (CRL-0012; CRL-0013; CRL-0010).

**5. Plaintiff's February 2026 complaint played no role in the decision.** The Company acknowledges that Plaintiff raised concerns about driver hours of service and dispatch scheduling practices in a January 12, 2026 email to Mr. Kestner and in a February 3, 2026 written complaint to HR (CRL-0002; CRL-0004). That complaint was logged as complaint 26-004, acknowledged in writing on February 4, 2026 with a reminder of the Company's no retaliation policy, and investigated by HR, including interviews of Mr. Kestner and Dispatch Supervisor Cody Brantley and a Safety and Compliance review of the relevant electronic logging device records. The review found that the driver on the January 8, 2026 Memphis run completed delivery 22 minutes inside his 14 hour window with no after the fact edits to duty status, that no hours of service violation was confirmed, and that December and January exception reports were consistent with prior quarters. The initial review was closed by memo to file on February 24, 2026 with a recommendation of dispatch refresher training on communication practices, and the outcome was communicated to Plaintiff (CRL-0005; CRL-0010; CRL-0018; CRL-0019). When Plaintiff asked at the April 6, 2026 termination meeting whether the termination was because of her February complaint, Mr. Kestner stated that it was not and that it was a structural decision (CRL-0010). Prior to the termination, the Company also sought and obtained legal advice from its General Counsel, Gregory Aldana, regarding the restructuring and the selection decision, including advice that the selection be made at the March 30 review on documented, objective criteria with the financial analysis before the group (CRL-0008; CRL-0010).
