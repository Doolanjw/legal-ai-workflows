This responds to your request for legal advice. Please keep this thread between us and do not forward it.

My advice is as follows. Tennessee recognizes retaliatory discharge claims at common law and under the Public Protection Act, and a termination coming a few weeks after a written compliance complaint will draw scrutiny on timing no matter how sound the business rationale is. That does not mean the company cannot proceed with a restructuring that is genuinely driven by the numbers. It means that if a decision to terminate is made, the record needs to show that the restructuring analysis stands on its own, that the selection criteria between the two positions are objective and documented, and that the February complaint played no role in the analysis. I recommend that any selection decision be made at the restructuring review Lena intends to convene, with the CFO's financial analysis in front of the group, that the criteria be written down at that meeting, and that nothing be communicated to any employee before the review is complete. Send me the draft separation documents before anything goes out. I would also like to talk through the severance release language given the potential statutory claim.

Call me if any of this is unclear.
